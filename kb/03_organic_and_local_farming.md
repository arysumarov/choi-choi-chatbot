# Organic Certification and Local Farming in Hong Kong

Sources: KFBG website, Central Farmers' Market page (checked 8 Nov 2026); facts and citations from the team's ITS2028 project proposal (Chu, 2026; AFCD, 2025; Lau, 2026; KFBG, n.d.).

## What does "HKORC certified" mean?
HKORC is the Hong Kong Organic Resource Centre. It runs an organic certification scheme for farms in Hong Kong. A farm that is HKORC certified has a certificate number (for example, C09001 for Zen Organic Farm).

In Hong Kong there is no law that sets organic standards, and HKORC certification is voluntary (Chu, 2026). This means a farm can choose whether to apply for certification.

## What does "self-claimed organic" mean?
A "self-claimed organic" farm says it farms organically, but it does not hold HKORC certification. At the Central Farmers' Market, two of the eight listed farms are self-claimed organic: Ming Yuen Farm and Cham Shan Farm. The other six are HKORC certified.

Choi Choi should not judge whether a self-claimed farm is "less organic". For questions about a farm's methods, visitors should ask the farmer at the stall.

## Why do customers trust this market?
In the team's fieldwork, customers said they trust the market because:
- the farms follow certification rules ("They follow the rules that they set up... more honest," said a young customer), and
- the market is organised by Kadoorie Farm and Botanic Garden.

## How much of Hong Kong's food is local?
- Local farms produce only about **1.7%** of the fresh vegetables eaten in Hong Kong (AFCD, 2025).
- The number of farmers in Hong Kong fell from about **120,000 in 1961** to about **4,300 in 2025**, mainly because of urbanisation and cheap imported food since the 1980s (AFCD, 2025; Lau, 2026).

## Why buy local organic vegetables at this market?
According to KFBG:
- It gives farmers a fairer return for their hard work (fair trade).
- Consumers get locally grown, organic, chemical-free greens.
- Local food travels a short distance, so it has much lower CO2 emissions.
- It supports the local economy and environmental protection.

## Questions Choi Choi should send to the farmers
Choi Choi does not answer these from its own knowledge. It suggests asking the farmer at the stall:
- Exact prices, or why one item costs more than another
- Whether a specific vegetable used any spray or pesticide
- Health or medical claims about a vegetable
- Comparisons between organic and non-organic food
