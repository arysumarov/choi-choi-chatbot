# Vegetable Nutrition Notes and Simple Home Recipes (10 種蔬菜 · 營養與家常做法)

Source: the team's "ChoiChoi 10 Vegetables Nutrition & Recipes" (2026). Written with elders in mind (soft, easy-to-eat cooking), so it's useful when a young visitor wants to cook for parents or grandparents.

How Choi Choi should use this file:
- Share nutrition notes as general information: "choy sum has calcium and vitamin C…". Do NOT promise that a vegetable prevents, treats or cures any illness.
- If someone asks about a specific illness, medicine or special diet (e.g. kidney disease, blood thinners, diabetes), say Choi Choi is a cabbage, not a doctor, and they should ask a doctor or dietitian.
- Recipes: give the short version and keep the Chinese dish name, e.g. 白灼菜心.

---

## 1. 菜心 Choy Sum
- **Nutrition:** one of the leafy greens with more calcium (good for bones), plus vitamin C, fibre and potassium. Low in calories, soft and easy to eat.
- **白灼菜心 Blanched choy sum (most recommended):** boil water with a pinch of salt and a few drops of oil, blanch choy sum 1–2 minutes until bright green, drain, then drizzle light soy sauce and a little hot garlic oil. Sweet, low-oil, keeps the nutrients.
- **蒜蓉炒菜心 Garlic stir-fried choy sum:** fry minced garlic until fragrant, stir-fry the choy sum on high heat 2–3 minutes, add a little salt. Stays crisp-tender.

## 2. 菠菜 Spinach
- **Nutrition:** folate, iron, vitamin K, potassium and lutein (a pigment found in the eye). It has quite a lot of oxalate, so blanch it first – that also helps your body use the calcium from other food.
- **菠菜蛋花湯 Spinach egg-drop soup:** blanch spinach 10–15 seconds, cut into sections. Boil water or clear broth, swirl in a beaten egg, add the spinach, a little salt and sesame oil. Soft and easy to swallow.
- **蒜蓉炒菠菜 Garlic spinach:** blanch first, then stir-fry quickly with garlic. Little oil, little salt, keep it bright green.

## 3. 西蘭花 Broccoli
- **Nutrition:** very high in vitamin C, rich in fibre, and contains sulforaphane, a natural plant compound that scientists are studying. Steamed or blanched until soft, it's easy to chew.
- **清蒸／焯水西蘭花 Steamed or blanched broccoli:** cut into small florets, steam or blanch 3–4 minutes until soft, drizzle a little light soy sauce or oyster sauce.
- **蒜蓉西蘭花 Garlic broccoli:** blanch first, then stir-fry quickly with garlic and a pinch of salt.

## 4. 紅蘿蔔 Carrot
- **Nutrition:** rich in β-carotene, which the body turns into vitamin A (needed for eyes and skin), plus fibre and potassium. Very soft when cooked. A little oil (stir-frying or stewing) helps the body absorb the carotene.
- **紅蘿蔔瘦肉湯 Carrot and lean pork soup:** carrot chunks, lean pork and ginger slices, simmered slowly until soft. Sweet and comforting.
- **清炒紅蘿蔔片 Stir-fried carrot slices:** slice thinly, stir-fry on high heat until soft, add a little salt. Can mix with other vegetables.

## 5. 南瓜 Pumpkin
- **Nutrition:** soft and easy to digest; has β-carotene, fibre and potassium; low in calories but filling.
- **清蒸南瓜 Steamed pumpkin:** peel, cut into chunks, steam 15–20 minutes until very soft. Eat plain or with a pinch of salt.
- **南瓜粥 Pumpkin congee:** cook pumpkin chunks with rice into a smooth congee – gentle for breakfast or dinner.

## 6. 白蘿蔔 Daikon / White Radish
- **Nutrition:** fibre and potassium, lots of water, low in calories; very easy to swallow when cooked soft. Especially popular in autumn and winter. In traditional Chinese food culture it's seen as a "cooling" food, so it's often cooked with meat to balance it.
- **白蘿蔔瘦肉湯 Daikon and lean pork soup:** daikon chunks, lean pork and ginger, simmered slowly until soft. Sweet and appetising.
- **清炒／燜白蘿蔔 Braised daikon:** cut into strips or chunks, braise with a little oil until soft, add a little salt or light soy sauce.

## 7. 番茄 Tomato
- **Nutrition:** rich in lycopene (absorbed better when cooked), plus vitamin C and potassium. Tangy-sweet taste helps appetite; very soft when cooked.
- **番茄蛋花湯 Tomato egg-drop soup:** stir-fry tomato chunks until juicy, add water, bring to the boil, swirl in beaten egg, add a little salt. A classic home dish.
- **番茄炒蛋 Tomato and scrambled egg:** cook the tomato until soft and juicy, then mix with scrambled egg. Little oil, little salt.

## 8. 茄子 Eggplant / Aubergine
- **Nutrition:** the purple skin contains anthocyanins (natural pigments); the flesh goes very soft when steamed or stewed. Low in calories.
- **清蒸茄子 Steamed eggplant:** cut into strips (or steam whole) until soft, then drizzle garlic and light soy sauce. Simple and light.
- **茄子煲 Braised eggplant:** slow-cook with a little lean pork or tofu until soft. Use little oil.

## 9. 芥蘭 Gai Lan / Kai-lan
- **Nutrition:** vitamins A and C, calcium and fibre. Its fibres are firmer, so cook it a bit softer for older people.
- **白灼芥蘭 Blanched gai lan:** blanch 2–3 minutes until soft, drizzle hot garlic oil or light soy sauce.
- **蒜蓉炒芥蘭 Garlic stir-fried gai lan:** stir-fry on high heat; blanch first if you want softer stems.

## 10. 冬瓜 Winter Melon
- **Nutrition:** almost all water and very low in calories; soft and silky when cooked; mild taste – perfect for soup. (Summer is winter-melon season – see the farmers' seasonal guide.)
- **冬瓜瘦肉湯 Winter melon and lean pork soup:** peel and cut the melon into chunks, simmer slowly with lean pork, ginger and dried shrimp (蝦米). Clear, sweet soup with soft melon.
- **清炒冬瓜 Stir-fried / braised winter melon:** slice thinly, stir-fry or braise with a little oil until soft, add a pinch of salt.
